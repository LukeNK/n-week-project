We could have defined animals as "species that move", but apparently that includes a lot of unrelated organisms while excludes other related species. That is why biologists came up with a clear definition of animals as organisms that:
- are multicellular where there are specialized cells with division of labour;
- have extracellular matrix;
- have collagen, which is a special protein that... do things; and
- have nervous system, which is unique to animals and allow them to coordinate responses to stimuli.

In animals, a group of specialized cells is called **a tissue**, a group of tissues to provide a function is called **an organ**, and a group of organs to perform a whole "assembly line" is called **an organ system**. This hierarchy is shared between animals and then we came up with the general **body plan** to categorize the species into their approproate phylum:

- **Symmetry**

Pretend that we divide an animal into two and both parts are similar to each other — we will call that **bilateral symmetry** ("bi" is two). Human is an example for an animal with bilateral symmetry. Another symmetry is **radial symmetry**, which can be found in jellyfishes. In simple words, they are circular and you can divide them into triangular parts.

- **Tissues**

We then move on to how **tissues** are arranged around the digestive tract. So in animals, our digestion happens by moving food through a tube and that tube is made of special tissues called endoderm. The outer-most layer of the body that directly exposes to the air (so basically the skin) is called ectoderm. If a species only have those two layers, it is **diploblastic**; if there is a mesoderm layer between those two layers, the species is **triboplastic**

- **Body cavity**

How the mesoderm fill the space between the inner and the outside "wall" of an animal can be another unique characteristic. Animals that have the space completely filled with mesoderm, we call it **acoelomate** (or compact). For the species that have some space in the mesoderm layer (that is usually filled with fluid), we call them **coelom**. On the "absolutely not" side of the spectrum, the species here only have the mesoderm layer attached to the ectodrem and the digestive tract is just freely suspended in the fluid — these species are called **hemoceol/pseudocoelom**.

- **Digestive cavity**

Since we have been talking about digestive tract, let's consider the route of the food. So in general, if the food comes into the "tube" and then waste comes out from the other end, we call that **alimentary canal**. If the food and waste go through the same hole, that species have **gastrovascular cavity**.

- **Embryonic evelopment**

During the development of the embryo of the species with alimentary canal, we can also consider if the mouth opening is developed first (**protostome**) or the anus (**deuterostome**, or mounth second).

- **Skeleton**

There are three different skeleton structure. The first one is simply using water pressure as a way to support the animal's body, which is called **hydrostatic skeleton**. For those that actually have a rigid external support structure, it is called **exoskeleton**. Lastly, we have endoskeleton, which means that the rigid structure are inside the animal.
