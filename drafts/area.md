### Area
Imagine you get a pencil, can you fill a shape with it by only using dots? You definitely can! That is the basic concept of area: it is how many dots you can fit into a shape. So **the area** of a shape is just a way numerical way for people to express how big it is.

So let's start with a square. How do we quickly count the number of dots in in our shape? We can use multiplication: we count the number of dots per line and then how many lines there are. However, "numerical" means it must be consistence. Therefore, mathematicians use the side lengths of the square as the basis for area, which gives it the general unit of $\text{length}\cdot\text{length}$.

### Area of basic shapes
As you can probably observe, it is quite easy to find the the area when the shape's two sides are perpendicular with each other. It is also especially easier for the square, where both sides are equal to each other. Because of that, **the area of the square** from <a href="#fig-m-square"></a> is:
<eq>
    \text{length}\times\text{length} = l \times l
</eq>

Rectangles is the basic shape for us to later construct the formula for the area of other shapes. Let the length be $l$ and height be $h$ (they can be any sides as long as they are two different sides), we have the **the area of the rectangle**:
<eq>
    \text{length}\times\text{height} = l \times h
</eq>
