### Characteristics
Fungi are chemoheterotroph. They are mostly multicellular except yeast, which decided to be the cool kid that breaks the rule and become unicellular. One unique characteristic of fungi is that they have cell walls that are made of chitin

One special thing about fungi is that they form branches that are called **hyphae** beneath the surface. This is why you cannot just "remove" food mold by scooping the top, because there is probably a whole network bellow the surface. The hyphae's function is similar to a plant's root: to penetrate bellow the surface and get nutrients. Some fungi also have hyphae specifically to feed on living animals, either by clinging on the skin or inserting itself to the skin cells to suck the nutrients.

When we talk about a fungus, we would usually think about a mushroom. In fact, a mushroom is a spore-producing structure above the ground, and fungi reproduce by sending **spore** (a single-cell reproductive structure) to other places.
