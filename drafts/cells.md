### Prokaryotic cell
Prokaryotic cells are tiny cells are mostly 1–4 micrometers in length. They mostly live independently (**unicellular**) or collaborate with each other (**colonial**). Colonial means that the cells are mostly similar to each other but by staying together, they ensure a higher chance of survival. Bacteria and archaea are structually prokaryotic.

All prokaryotic cells are surround by **cell walls**. This is a rigid layer that surround the cells. Inside that layer is another softer enclosure called **plasma membrane**. The plasma membrane is constructed of a phospholipid bilayer to create a hydrophobic region that prevents water from entering in while oxygen and certain nutrients can pass through it. However, other special packages must pass through the security gates called **membrane proteins**.

To refer to all of the things inside the cell membrane, biologists called them **cytoplasm**. So first and foremost, the cytoplasm is filled with water (**cytosol**), which allow things to be "floaty" and easily move between places inside a cell. Because of that, all cell functions of a prokaryotic cell occur directly in the cytosol. Even the DNA are just floating around the **nucleoid**, which is basically the name fothe region where the cell's DNA is located.

### Eukaryotic cell
Eukaryotic cells are larger than prokaryotic cell in size because most are about 10–100 micrometers. They can be unicellular or colonial like prokaryotic cells, but they can also work in a **multicellular organisms**. Compared to colonial, the cells here can be specialized in one specific task in a bigger organism. That also means that they are highly dependent on each other and cannot survive on their own. All creatures in the Eukarya domain have eykaryotic cells.

All eukaryotic cell also have **cell membrane**, but only some cells have **cell walls** (such as plants or fungi). So for the cells without cell walls, you can imagine they are like water baloons being put into a big box.

In contrast to prokaryotic cells, the cell functions are **compartmentalized** where there are **organelles** to do a specific tasks. In this arrangement, the DNA is located inside the **nucleus**, which is basically a room warped by double-membrane.
