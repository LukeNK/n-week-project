### What are plants?
One of the closest neighbor of plants are multicellular protists (seaweed). Therefore, biologists define plants based on certain characteristics that its closest relative (green algae) do not have — these are called **shared derived traits of plants**:
- plants alternative between two distinct multicellular life stages;
- plants's spores are stored in sporangia;
- plants have multicellular gametangias, which is the productive reproductive organ to store sperms and eggs; and
- plants have apical meristems, which is an area of rapid cell production for growing.

Plants also require some unique **resources** for growing, which can be grouped into three major functions:
- Photosynthesis (generate glucos and oxygen): water, carbon dioxide, and light;
- Cellular respiration (generate ATP): oxygen; and
- General growth: mineral nutrients.

### Key adapations
Note that in the part above, we slightly touched about the reproduction of plants. However, what is it exactly? That lies in the two main **reproductive key adaptations**. These two characteristics serve the purpose of increasing the chance of reproduction.
- **Pollen (male gamette dispersal)**: it is a male reproductive structure that can be transported by the wind, allowing longer distance disperal.
- **Seed (offspring dispersal)**: a multicellular structure that houses the offspring; consist of the embryo, food supply, and seed coat.

**Pollen** is a type of special enclosure of sperms. Under water, sperms can easily swim to fertilize eggs; on land, the environment is much dryer, so the first land plants must make this key adaptation to utilize the new mean of transportation available: wind. The wind can spreads the pollen so a larger area, increasing the chance of fertilization. However, that is not enough, so some plant also adapt to increase the ability to disperse pollen through insects with **flowers**. We can easily imagine how flowers can be attractive to insects: the colour, the scent, and the sweet juice to reward those who help the plants to spread the pollen. Even humans are attracted to flowers! Such "direct delivery" method reduces the chance of "missing packages" and inmprove the reproduction.

So the pollen allow plants to create a seed and that seed can detach to form a new plant. You might wonder: what comes _before_ seed? It is **spore**. In fact, some groups of plants still use spore as their mean of reproduction. Both seed and spore have a protective coat to survire extreme environment, but only seeds can offer these two advantages:
- jump start the development because they have multicellular embryo; and
- initial resource for growing offspring from the food supply.

Another key adaptation following seeds are **fruits**. This swollen ovary (soft layer) protects the seed to increase the seed's chance of survival. It also increase the dispersal distance because animals can carry the seeds to other places, which also decreases the competition between offsprings.

Structually, plants also become more rigid after coming to land. It is quite easy to deduct why: under water, the natural boyancy of the protists will allow it to flow and "stand" upwards, helping with light capture; on land, the plants have to rise up by themselves or risk living their entire life in the shadow.
- **Non-vascular plants:** no root, stem, or leaves system; rely on cell-to-cell transport; size being limited to absorbing the resources in close vicinity.
- **Vascular plants:** have an efficient root-to-shoot transport system; the root have access to water while the leaves have access to sunlight; much larger in size because resources can be transported much further.
