### Taxonomy
There are a lot of spiecies in our planet, but it is unfortunate that we have not able to study them all. Most organisms are small and easily overlooked, while others live in hard to access locations. Sometimes, diffrent species look alike. Therefore, we need a system for naming and classifying organisms so that we can document them, and some of the most widely adopted system is the **Linnaean taxonomy** system. This will ensure that scientists from everywhere in the world can communicate about the spieces they are researching.

The **taxonomic hierarchy** starts at the species level and organize them into larger groups based on **morphological traits**. That means that scpieces in the same group will have similar physical characteristics. So from the largest to the smallest, we have:
1. Domain → most inclusive
2. Kingdom
3. Phylum
4. Class
5. Order
6. Family
7. Genus
8. Species → least inclusive.

So to refer to a member of any level of the hierarchy, we use the word **taxon** (plural **taxa**). As for the basic, the **three dmomain of life** are Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukarya.

Of course, it is impossible for us to say the entire hierarchy every time refer to a species. Therefore, we use **Bionomial Nomenclature**, which is a two-part format for assigning scientific names to species. In this format, we simply refer to a species with its genus and species class. So human will be referred to as _Homo sapiens_.

### Phylogeny
When we talk about similarities between two spiecies, we mostly use **morphological** similarities. This means that the similarities are based on shapes and forms. This means that there are physical similarities between two spiecies, such as the same number of bones or skin characteristics. Usuaully, having the same traits would mean having the same DNA, which is called **molecular** similarities.

Biologists wanted to go a step further and categorize _how_ a similarity arise, so they call the similarities between two species due to shared ancestry "**homologies**". Sometimes, there are also similar traits that occur because the environment force two different species to develop the same characteristics, which are called **analogies**.

### Phylogenetic tree
A **phylogenetic tree** shows us the hypothesized timeline between the taxa and when they stopped being similar to teach other. In a tree:
- **the branch tips** represent the species that are currently exist;
- **the nodes** represent the points where an acestor split to form new taxa; and
- **the root** shows the common ancestor of all the taxa in the tree.

To determine how "close" two taxa are, you simply consider "what is the most recent common ancestor between them?" When when you follow the path between them, just find the lowest point in the graph and remember that: you cannot walk on the same path twice.

When there is a branching happening, the taxa will share some traits that are similar to the ancestor, which is called **shared ancestral traits**. However, there will be also a unique difference give us the reason to classify that species as a different unit; that difference is what we called as a "**shared derived trait**".
