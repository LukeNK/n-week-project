### Splitting fraction
The first step you need to do is factoring the numerator.

Now, there are two ways for you to split the fraction, depending on what you have. We will need to go through each factor and consider the highest power: if it is a degree one or two.

Note that we always consider what is inside the bracket, and the exponent outside the bracket will tell us how many times we will need to repeat the partial fraction terms.

### Finding the solution
After all of that efforts to split the fraction, we will now combine it again. Here, it is important to remember one important aspect of fraction addition:
<eq>
    \frac{A}{B} + {C}{D} = \frac{A\cdot D + C\cdot B}{B\cdot D}
</eq>

Wait what? Why are we combining everything back again? Actually, it is because now, we got a completing new fraction that is equal to the old fraction. More importantly, we can now equate the new numerator to the old numerator.
